Behind the postcards

About this website

Nothing Happened Here is a collection of small travel moments: an open door, a quiet path, flowers by the street, water reflecting the sky. I chose these images because they are easy to pass over. They are not always the famous view or the dramatic landmark, but they are often the details that stay with me after a trip ends.

The project began with Wallace Stevens’s poem The House Was Quiet and the World Was Calm. I was interested in how the poem is not only about deep reading; it also asks the reader to read deeply. It cannot really be rushed. The poem creates a quiet space where attention matters, and that feeling became the starting point for this website.

Mindfulness

I also thought about mindfulness through the idea of living in the present moment. When I travel, I try to practice that by paying attention to what is already around me instead of only searching for something impressive. A place is not only what it looks like. It is also what it sounds like, how the air feels, and what I notice when I stop moving for a second.

That is why some postcards include ambient sound. The sounds are not meant to explain the photograph. They help recreate how I remember the moment. A wave, a path, or a quiet outdoor space can make a place feel present again in a way that an image alone cannot.

Slowing down online

We usually look at images online very quickly. We scroll, glance, and move on. I wanted this website to resist that habit in a small way. The postcard format asks you to turn each image over, read a short note, and spend a little more time with one ordinary moment.

The idea is simple, maybe even a little cliché, but it feels important now because so much of our attention is pulled apart by the internet. I wanted to build a website that is about mindfulness, and also requires a little mindfulness from the person using it.
